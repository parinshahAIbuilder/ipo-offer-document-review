# Reconciliation tests

Recalculate. Do not accept the document's own totals, subtotals, percentages or
ratios. The single most productive habit in this work is re-footing a table the
issuer expects nobody to re-foot.

Work through these in order. The early ones are cheap and catch most defects;
the later ones need more of the document read first.

Show the arithmetic in the report. A finding with the calculation visible is one
the issuer can check and either concede or rebut on the merits. A finding
without it invites a denial.

---

## A. Foot everything

For each primary statement and each summary table:

1. Do the components sum to the stated total? Check every subtotal, not just
   the bottom line.
2. Does the balance sheet balance? Total assets against total equity plus total
   liabilities, each year.
3. Does profit or loss attributable to owners plus non controlling interests
   equal total profit or loss? Same for total comprehensive income.
4. Does opening equity plus movements equal closing equity, line by line in the
   statement of changes in equity?
5. Does closing cash in the cash flow statement equal cash on the balance
   sheet, each year? And does opening plus net movement plus any translation
   reserve equal closing?

**The highest yield test in the whole battery** is comparing the Summary of
Financial Information against the restated statements line by line. The summary
is prepared separately, often late, and is where the same figure appears twice
with two values. Check every line, not a sample.

---

## B. Cash flow decomposition

Reconcile profit after tax through to the change in cash, and then ask what is
actually driving the operating line.

```
Profit before tax
  + non cash adjustments        (depreciation, ECL, share based payment,
                                 gains and losses on disposal, lease
                                 modifications, unrealised fair value moves)
  = operating profit before working capital
  +/- each working capital movement
  = cash generated from operations
  - taxes paid
  = net cash from operating activities
```

Then compute, for the latest year:

- **The share of operating cash flow contributed by the single largest working
  capital movement.** If one line is more than half the total, the headline is
  not a measure of operating performance and the report should say so with the
  adjusted figure alongside.
- **Cash conversion**, being operating cash flow divided by profit after tax,
  on both the headline and the adjusted basis.
- **Capital expenditure in the cash flow against capital expenditure disclosed
  in Objects of the Issue.** These are prepared by different teams and diverge
  more often than they should.

Red flags worth stating explicitly: operating cash flow persistently below
profit after tax; a large positive swing in one year driven by a single
liability; financing inflows funding operating shortfalls; taxes paid diverging
persistently from the tax charge.

## C. Basis for the issue price

Recompute all of it. This section is arithmetic and either agrees or does not.

- **Weighted average EPS and RoNW.** Sum of (year figure x weight) divided by
  sum of weights. Weights are normally 3, 2, 1 for the three years.
- **Net worth.** Take equity attributable to owners and subtract the reserves
  the definition excludes, which normally means revaluation reserve, share
  based payment reserve, capital redemption reserve and debenture redemption
  reserve. Confirm the result equals the disclosed net worth **to the rupee**,
  and confirm it excludes non controlling interests. This is a genuine test: a
  net worth that quietly includes a revaluation reserve overstates net asset
  value per share and understates return on net worth.
- **RoNW** against net worth, and **NAV per share** against the closing share
  count implied by it.
- **Every percentage in the peer table**, and the P/E figures against the
  disclosed closing price and EPS.
- **Contingent liabilities as a percentage of net worth**, each line and the
  total.

Watch for two different return measures presented as returns on shareholders'
funds within the same section, computed on different denominators or averaging
conventions. Both may be correctly defined and still mislead when placed pages
apart without a cross reference.

Where the peer set is a single company, or is loss making so the P/E is
negative, the table gives no usable benchmark. Say so plainly rather than
repeating a number that cannot be used.

## D. Key performance indicators

Recompute every KPI from the restated statements. Definitions are given in the
notes to the KPI table; use those definitions, not the conventional ones, and
say where a definition departs from convention.

- EBITDA: normally profit before tax plus depreciation and amortisation plus
  finance costs less other income. Reconstruct it and confirm it agrees.
- Margins: each against the revenue figure actually used.
- Days sales outstanding: closing receivables divided by revenue times days.
  **Also use this in reverse to confirm the column order of the receivables
  table**, which is the cheapest defence against reading a table backwards.
- Return on equity and return on capital employed: check whether the
  denominator is opening, closing or average, and whether it reconciles.
- Debt service coverage: many issuers define it as EBITDA divided by current
  borrowings, which is not the conventional earnings available for debt service
  divided by interest plus principal. A non standard definition is permitted if
  disclosed, but if the Schedule III ratio note shows a different figure for the
  same year, that is the same data with two values and is a finding.
- Average revenue per customer against total customers and revenue.
- Any percentage split, such as revenue by vertical or by service line, should
  sum to 100 in each year. Convert the percentages back to absolute amounts and
  look for a vertical that grew as a percentage while shrinking in rupees, or
  the reverse. That is where the story is.

## E. Earnings quality

Identify anything that flatters the latest year or the comparative years used
in pricing, and quantify it against profit before tax:

- Non recurring gains: lease modification or termination gains, gains on
  disposal, reversal of provisions, balances written back, government grants.
  These often appear only as an adjustment line in the cash flow statement,
  never separately identified in the profit and loss.
- **Changes in accounting estimates**, above all the expected credit loss
  charge. Compute the charge as a percentage of closing receivables for each
  year. A sharp fall in the year immediately before an offer, especially while
  receivables rise, is worth a query with the profit effect quantified.
- Capitalisation of costs that were previously expensed, and movements in
  capital work in progress that are large relative to the depreciation charge.
- Effective tax rate by year, against 25.168 per cent and 34.944 per cent.
- Related party revenue or expense as a proportion of the total.

Present an indicative adjusted profit before tax with the adjustments listed,
and label it clearly as indicative and as a sensitivity rather than an assertion
that the reported figures are wrong.

---

## F. Objects of the issue

- Sum of object amounts plus issue expenses plus general corporate purposes
  against gross proceeds.
- General corporate purposes against the 25 per cent cap in Regulation 7(2).
- Deployment schedule: does it sum to the object amount, by year and by item?
- **Look at the ratios between years for each line item.** If every category
  splits across the years in an identical ratio, the schedule was derived top
  down from a total rather than built up from quotations, whatever the stated
  basis says. That is worth raising when the document also concedes that orders
  have not been placed.
- Proposed spend against historical spend on the same category over the
  preceding three years. A large multiple deserves a query about execution
  capacity.
- Any asset, facility or subsidiary that features in the growth narrative but is
  excluded from the Objects: how is it being funded?
- Cash and cash equivalents held against the amount being raised. If the company
  already holds more than it is asking for, the document must explain why, and
  usually the answer is that the cash is matched by a liability. Compute the
  unencumbered figure and check whether the document performs that reconciliation
  anywhere.

---

## G. Capital structure and per share figures

- Build the share count forward from incorporation through each allotment,
  bonus, split and buy back, and confirm it reaches the pre issue count.
- Confirm bonus issues and splits have been applied retrospectively to every
  earnings per share figure, as Ind AS 33 paragraph 64 requires.
- Weighted average share count against the EPS disclosed.
- Pre issue placements: price per share against the securities premium recorded,
  and against the eventual price band once known.
- Promoter contribution lock in, minimum promoter contribution, and the
  eligibility of the securities offered towards it.

---

## H. Related party

- Aggregate related party transactions as a proportion of revenue, expenses and
  net worth.
- Each director's remuneration against sections 197 and 198, and against the
  sub limits, being 5 per cent for a single managing or whole time director,
  10 per cent for all of them together, 1 per cent for other directors where a
  managing or whole time director is in office, and 11 per cent overall.
- Loans and advances to subsidiaries: interest rate implied by the interest
  income against the average balance, and whether the arrangement is covered by
  section 186 approvals.
- Any entity that ceased to be a related party during the period, which usually
  signals a disposal whose accounting and counterparty need checking.
- Whether closing balances, terms, security and settlement basis are disclosed,
  not merely transaction values.
